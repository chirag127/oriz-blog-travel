# A 12-day budget Himalayan itinerary from Delhi, under ₹25,000

Most Himalaya itineraries you find online quietly assume a car, a booked-ahead
hotel chain, and a flight into Kullu. This one assumes none of that. It is the
route I actually log when a friend messages "I have twelve days and about
twenty-five thousand rupees — where do I go?"

The answer, most years, is the Parvati Valley loop: Delhi → Kasol → Tosh →
Kheerganga → Manikaran → back. It is cheap because it runs on public buses and
guesthouses, it is beautiful in a way that does not need editing, and it does
not require permits or guides for the legs below.

## The route at a glance

```
Delhi ●━━━━━━━● Bhuntar ●━━━● Kasol ●━━━● Tosh
                                     ┊
                          Kheerganga ○ (trek, 1 night)
                                     ┊
                          Manikaran ○━━━━━━━━━● back to Delhi
```

- **Legs 1–2 (Delhi → Kasol):** overnight Volvo/HRTC bus, ~10–12 hours.
- **Legs 3–5 (valley hopping):** local buses and shared taxis, 30–90 min each.
- **Leg 6 (Kheerganga trek):** a real 12 km day hike, one night at the top.

Twelve days is generous for this loop. The slack is deliberate — weather turns,
buses run late, and the point of a valley like this is to not rush it.

## Where the money actually goes

Here is the honest breakdown for one person, off-season shoulder months
(late September or May), staying in dorms and shared rooms:

- **Transport, round trip Delhi–valley–Delhi:** ₹2,500–3,500 on buses.
- **Local hops inside the valley:** ₹800–1,200 total.
- **Accommodation, 11 nights:** ₹400–700 a night in Kasol and Tosh
  guesthouses; ₹300 in the basic Kheerganga camps. Call it ₹6,000.
- **Food, 12 days:** ₹350–500 a day if you eat where locals and long-stay
  travellers eat, not the Israeli-menu cafes on the main strip. ₹5,000.
- **Kheerganga trek:** free to walk; ₹300–500 for the night at the top.

That lands around ₹16,000–19,000. The ₹25,000 ceiling leaves genuine room for a
splurge meal, a day of doing nothing on a riverside deck, or the bad-weather
buffer you will be glad you kept.

## Day by day

**Days 1–2 — Delhi to Kasol.** Take the evening bus from Kashmiri Gate or
Majnu Ka Tila. Book the HRTC ordinary or a private Volvo a day ahead in peak
months; walk-up is fine off-season. You arrive at Bhuntar around dawn, catch
the local bus up the valley, and are in Kasol by mid-morning. Sleep. Walk the
river. Do nothing ambitious.

**Days 3–4 — Kasol and Chalal.** Chalal is a flat 30-minute walk across the
river from Kasol and costs nothing. It is quieter, cheaper to sleep in, and a
good place to reset your sense of what a fair room rate is before you climb
higher.

**Days 5–6 — Tosh.** Bus to Barshaini, then the short uphill walk into Tosh.
This is the postcard leg — the village hangs off the mountainside and the
guesthouses higher up have the views. Rooms are cheaper the more you climb.

**Days 7–8 — Kheerganga trek.** From Barshaini it is roughly 12 km up to the
hot springs at Kheerganga, four to six hours depending on your pace and the
weather. Carry water, a headlamp, and layers; the temperature drops hard after
sunset. Sleep in a camp at the top, soak in the springs, walk down the next
morning.

**Days 9–10 — Manikaran and Kasol.** Manikaran's gurudwara serves free langar
and has natural hot springs used to cook the community meal. It is a genuinely
moving stop and it costs nothing. Drift back to Kasol for your last valley
nights.

**Days 11–12 — Back to Delhi.** Reverse the opening leg. Take the night bus
from Bhuntar so you lose a sleeping night to travel, not a daylight one.

## What to carry, what to skip

Carry: layers you can peel, a rain shell, a headlamp, a power bank, cash in
small notes (ATMs thin out fast above Kasol), and a basic first-aid kit with
rehydration salts. Skip: a big pack, a fixed hotel plan, and the assumption
that you will have signal. Jio and Airtel work in Kasol and patchily in Tosh;
above Barshaini, assume nothing.

The valley rewards travellers who arrive without a rigid plan and leave with a
better one. Log your own version as you go — the route above is a starting
line, not a script.
